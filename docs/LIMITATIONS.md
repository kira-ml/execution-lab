# Limitations

The lab is useful for ranking and sensitivity. It is not a production execution
simulator, and it is not evidence of tradable alpha. The limitations below are
part of the result, not a disclaimer bolted on at the end.

---

## 1. Daily data cannot see the intraday market

The model uses daily OHLCV. It cannot observe:

- quoted spreads at the time of the order;
- order-book depth or queue position;
- intraday volume bursts;
- the path of the price during execution;
- whether a passive order would have filled.

The spread is a high-low proxy or an observed daily series. The intraday volume
profile is a stylised U-shape. Neither is estimated from this dataset.

**Consequence:** the model can rank schedules under a common assumption, but it
cannot predict the cost of a specific order on a specific day.

---

## 2. `k` is not identified

The square-root coefficient is a free parameter. Daily data alone cannot pin it
down. Reporting a single `k` would be false precision.

**What the lab does instead:** a grid, an implied `k` for observed costs, and a
break-even `k` at which the strategy's edge or ranking changes. The break-even is
the decision-relevant number.

---

## 3. The impact model is stylised

`impact = k · σ · participation^0.5` is a reduced-form approximation. It omits:

- permanent vs temporary impact decomposition;
- order-book shape and replenishment;
- cross-asset impact and correlation;
- intraday volatility seasonality;
- strategic response by other traders.

The square-root law is a useful regularisation, not a law of nature. A linear
exponent is available as a falsification case.

---

## 4. Timing risk is measured against arrival, not VWAP

The timing-risk term is the standard deviation of the implementation shortfall
relative to the arrival price. A trader benchmarked to VWAP would measure a
different risk, often much smaller for a VWAP schedule. The model's risk-aversion
parameter is therefore not comparable across benchmarks.

**Consequence:** the optimal schedule depends on the benchmark and the risk
aversion. The demo reports the frontier rather than declaring one schedule
universally best.

---

## 5. Expected cost, not a simulated path

The model prices expected cost and a risk standard deviation. It does not
simulate price paths, fills, or partial fills. The 95% cost and CVaR figures are
normal approximations, not empirical tails.

---

## 6. The public demo is synthetic

The six-ETF panel is generated by a stylised factor model. Its strategy
dispersion, correlations, and volume levels are chosen to exercise the
accounting, not to match real ETFs.

**Consequence:** the demo's Sharpe levels and ranking are not empirical claims.
The real-data check (separate document, aggregate results only) is the closest
the project comes to an empirical statement, and it is still a daily-data
exercise.

---

## 7. No live execution

There is no broker connection, order router, FIX session, or trading platform.
The project does not place orders. It has no opinion on whether a strategy should
be traded.

---

## 8. Counter-metrics are mandatory

Do not report only “lower cost.” A lower-cost schedule can create more timing
risk, require more turnover, depend on unavailable data, or fail at scale. Pair
cost with:

- timing risk;
- turnover;
- ranking sensitivity;
- capacity (AUM at which the edge crosses zero);
- implementation complexity.

The report does this. A reader who takes only the cost column is misusing the
tool.
